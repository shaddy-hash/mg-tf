The car

A genuine TF 1250 built for the North American market, in one family since it was nearly new.

My father was this car's second owner, and it has stayed with us ever since. He died in 2014, and I transferred the title out of his name and into mine in 2023 — which is why the Nebraska title shows a June 2023 purchase date and an Iowa previous title. I am selling because the car deserves more use than I am giving it.

It is finished in British Racing Green over black and rides on chrome wire wheels. The firewall plate, the stamping on the engine block, and the brass plate under the scuttle lid all agree with one another, and all three are photographed in the gallery below.

The car has never been modernized. There is no five-speed conversion, no engine swap, no disc brake conversion, and no negative-ground rewire. A Pertronix electronic ignition and an electronic fuel pump were fitted in 2020 — both common, reversible reliability upgrades — and the car remains positive ground as MG built it.

It was repainted at some point from the original Ivory, then yellow, and now British Racing Green. At a few panel seams the earlier yellow is still visible, and that is photographed too.

A note on the odometer

The odometer reads 2,854 and the Nebraska title records the odometer as exempt, as Nebraska exempts pre-1981 vehicles. No mileage claim is made or implied. A five-digit odometer on a car of this age has almost certainly rolled over more than once.

Is this a TF 1250 or a TF 1500?

A 1250. The engine prefix XPAG confirms it; the later TF 1500 used the XPEG engine.

Condition, in full

This is a 72-year-old driver, not a show car. I would rather you read all of this now than find it in my driveway.

Mechanically the car is sound. The brakes are firm, hold without sinking, and stop it straight. It holds temperature on hot days. The clutch and all four gears work properly. The handbrake works. It is a pleasant car to drive.

Paint and body

The paint is a driver-quality older respray, not a show finish. There is fine surface scratching across most panels; when I had it detailed the shop advised against buffing it out because it would take paint with it, so it has been left alone. There is wear at the corners of both doors, and rock chips on the front and rear fairings from years of being driven on a gravel road at my father's place. At a few panel seams the earlier yellow paint is visible underneath.

There is no significant corrosion anywhere on the body or the frame. The floors and the sills are good.

Chrome

All the chrome is original and none of it has been redone. Most of it has light scaling or pitting — the grille, the headlight rims, the bumpers and overriders, the turn signals, the mirrors, the windscreen frame, the door handles, and the boss of the steering wheel. It is not heavy, but it is there on nearly every piece. The luggage rack is the exception and is clean.

Interior and top

The seats are good with no tears. One of the two door pockets, on the passenger side, has been chewed through — a rodent got to it at some point. The convertible top is in good condition, goes up and down, and fits. The rear window and the side curtains have yellowed heavily with age, though all the side curtain frames and hardware are present and they install as they should.

Electrical faults

Three, all minor and all likely easy fixes: the tachometer does not work, the three gauge lights do not work so the dash is hard to read at night, and the speedometer reads roughly 7 to 10 mph slow.

The oil pressure, water temperature, ammeter and fuel gauges all work. The headlights, turn signals, brake lights, tail lights and license plate light all work, as does the manual wiper.

Drips

Like most T-series cars, it marks its spot. There are slow drips of both oil and coolant — nothing running, just the occasional drop on the floor after it sits.

Wheels and tires

There are chips in the silver paint on the wire wheels. The tires were fitted new in August 2020 and have covered under 500 miles since; the tread is essentially unworn. The DOT date codes are weeks 15 and 16 of 2018 and week 24 of 2019. I have not replaced them. A new owner may want to on age alone.

Gearbox

There is no synchromesh on first gear, so the car needs a full stop before selecting it. That is how MG built it, not a fault.
